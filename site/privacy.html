Privacy

Last updated November 22, 2026

HotSonos is a Windows tray app that runs on your own PC. It has no HotSonos account, no HotSonos server, no analytics, no ads, and no telemetry. The developer never receives your data.

What stays on your PC

HotSonos keeps its data in %LocalAppData%\HotSonos on your computer, including:

  • Settings (settings.json), including hotkeys, speaker groups, and saved Spotify links you paste in
  • Shuffle play history (play-history.json) so recent songs aren't repeated
  • Reports and activity log (activity.db) — song plays, skips, and speaker events, kept up to 180 days
  • A cache of your music library's metadata (library.db)
  • Tracks heard from Spotify, radio, or other non-library sources (heard-outside-library.json, up to 500 tracks / 90 days)
  • A play lifecycle trail used for troubleshooting (play-events.jsonl)
  • Log files (logs)

Uninstalling HotSonos and deleting that folder removes all of it.

Who HotSonos talks to

  • Your Sonos speakers, on your local network. HotSonos discovers and controls them directly over the LAN. Album art is loaded from the address your speaker reports for the current track.
  • Your music share. To build the library cache and write tags into your music files, HotSonos reads (and, for tagging, writes) the network share your Sonos system already indexes.
  • Your music services, only through your speakers. Music from Spotify, radio, and other services is played by your speakers using the accounts already linked in the Sonos app. HotSonos does not ask for those passwords.
  • Saved Spotify links. Spotify share links you paste on the Control tab are stored in your settings. Playing one tells your speaker to play it with the Spotify account already linked in the Sonos app; HotSonos does not contact Spotify for this.
  • Optional Spotify account link (debug tool). If you use HotSonos's debug tool to link Spotify for playlist browsing, HotSonos talks to the Spotify music-service address your Sonos speaker reports, and stores the resulting session token locally on your PC. This is off unless you start it.

Local MCP server (optional)

When turned on, the MCP endpoint for AI assistants listens only on this PC's loopback address (127.0.0.1). Other devices on your network can't reach it. Any AI assistant you connect to it is governed by that assistant's own privacy policy.

Android app (remote or standalone)

The Android app, including on a Chromebook, works in two ways. As a remote, it connects to the HotSonos tray app on your PC over your local network and sends a 6-digit house PIN, shown in the PC tray app, with every request. The PC rejects requests without the correct PIN. The phone stores only the PC address and PIN in the app's private storage. The remote listener on the PC can be turned off in Options, and the firewall rule HotSonos offers to add applies to Private networks only. Standalone, the app talks to your Sonos speakers on the same Wi-Fi and does not contact the PC. It does not use a HotSonos account or a HotSonos server.

iPhone and iPad: early prototype.

This website

hotsonos.com is a static site with no cookies, trackers, or analytics. Like any web server, it may keep standard access logs (such as IP address and pages requested) for security and troubleshooting. Download links go to GitHub, which has its own privacy policy.
